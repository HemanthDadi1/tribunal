Who settles it? Simulating an agent marketplace

Shopping agents buy refurbished laptops and travel agents book hotels, around the clock. Some sellers' agents misdescribe what they sell. The same market runs twice: once with today's dispute handling (support tickets and chargebacks) and once with Tribunal (escrow, a three-judge panel, human appeals and reputation).

Assumptions

Illustrative figures for exploring the idea, not measured data
  • 40 laptop sellers and 40 hotels. Honest sellers have a problem on 3% of deals; misrepresenting sellers on 35%, and are 30% more likely to be picked today because they list lower prices.
  • Laptops cost ₹15,000–60,000; hotel stays ₹2,000–9,000 a night for 1–3 nights.
  • Laptop problems: dead on arrival (40%, full refund) or battery below promise (60%, 12% refund). Hotel problems: no room (40%, full refund) or a missing amenity such as the pool (60%, 25% refund).
  • Today, buyers pursue 35% of problems under ₹5,000 and 70% above. A claim takes 7–45 days and costs the platform ₹600 to handle. Buyers win 75% with good evidence, 40% with poor, and 30% of false claims succeed.
  • With Tribunal, agents file 97% of problems automatically. Each of three judges is right 92% of the time with good evidence and 72% with poor, and abstains 30% of the time on poor evidence. A ruling costs ₹30.
  • Splits go to a human (95% right, 2–5 days, ₹1,500). Losers appeal 60% of wrong rulings and 5% of right ones; appeals use the same human review.
  • Buyer agents avoid sellers with a high rate of lost rulings. Sellers losing over 20% of 20+ deals are suspended.
  • Money in escrow can't be withdrawn before the dispute window closes, so a refund always has funds behind it.
